My story

It started with a screwdriver.

Apparently, I had a habit of taking my toys apart as a kid just to figure out how they worked. Putting them back together? That was another story.

I grew up when the internet still felt like a whole new world. I remember exploring sites like Neopets, tinkering with Pentium computers and thinking Windows XP was the best thing ever. Video games were what first pulled me in, but before long, I wanted to build things of my own. I started making websites and even set up a Ragnarok Online server. It didn't have many players, but I didn't mind. Half the fun was learning Linux, figuring out networking and getting the whole thing running.

After studying Computer Science, I started my career in tech support, digging through logs, debugging issues and tracing problems back to their source. I liked figuring out what went wrong, but I wanted to build the software, too. It took a couple of years to get that chance, and I kept working toward it.

Once I moved into development, I kept challenging myself through side projects, books and every new role I took on. Eventually, I became a Technical Architect. I'm proud of the trust I've earned and the work it took to get here.

That urge to figure things out never really went away. These days, if a project calls for a tool I don't have or a skill I haven't learned, I'd rather learn how to do it myself. I've collected quite a few tools that way—and I'm a lot better at putting things back together now.

Engineering & leadership

How I work.

I spend a lot of time thinking about the decisions behind the code: what will work now, what might cause trouble later, and how to make things easier for the people working on it.

01

Seeing the whole picture.

When I’m designing something, I don’t stop at the application code. I think about how it connects to APIs, data, infrastructure and deployment—and whether the team will be able to maintain it without headaches later.

⌘
02

Helping developers grow.

I enjoy being someone developers can bounce ideas off. When someone is stuck, I’d rather help them work through the problem than just hand over an answer. I want people to feel confident making decisions on their own.

↗
03

Getting to the root of it.

Some of my favourite work starts with a bug nobody can quite explain. I follow it wherever it leads—code, databases, integrations or servers—until I understand what’s really happening.

□
04

Keeping things practical.

I don’t think every problem needs a big redesign. Sometimes the better choice is a smaller change that’s easier to understand, deliver and maintain.

In practice

A few problems I’ve helped solve.

Most of my professional work happens behind the scenes, but here are three results I can share without naming clients.

20 min → milliseconds

From minutes to milliseconds

A page was taking roughly 20 minutes to load. I traced the delay to oversized data transfer objects and reworked them to bring the load time down to milliseconds.

30% → 80%+

Better test coverage, together

My team raised JUnit coverage from around 30% to more than 80%. That’s a result I’m proud to have been part of.

500+ stores

Automating flyer data

I helped automate digital flyer data for a retail platform serving more than 500 stores, making a repetitive integration easier to manage.
