My story

It started with a screwdriver.

Apparently, I took my toys apart as a kid to see how they worked. Putting them back together was another story.

Games got me into computers. I built my own PC, taught myself HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and hosted a Ragnarok Online server. It didn’t have many players, but half the fun was learning Linux, sorting out the networking, and getting the thing to stay online.

After studying Computer Science, I started in technical support. I liked digging through logs and tracing a problem back to its source. I also wanted to be the person who could change the software that caused it. I kept learning alongside the day job until I got my chance to move into development.

That eventually led me to technical architecture. I’m proud of the trust I’ve earned along the way, especially when a developer I’ve worked with starts making decisions they once would have brought to me.

My approach

How I work.

Follow the evidence.

For an unfamiliar production issue, I start with a concrete example and follow it through the logs, data, and dependencies. I want to be able to explain why the failure happened and how we can tell when it’s fixed.

Write down the trade-offs.

I compare options against how the system is deployed, what the team can support, and how much we can change safely. An incremental change can buy time to learn before committing to a larger redesign.

Leave the reasoning with the team.

I use design and code reviews to explain why a choice made sense and invite someone to challenge it. That shared context matters when the next developer has to extend the design or deal with it in production.
