My story

Before it was a career, it was a hobby.

Video games led me to building websites and running gaming servers with friends. I wanted to understand what was happening behind the screen.

My first professional role was in technical support. I could have stayed somewhere familiar, but I wanted to write software, so I moved into development. Over time, I took on larger engineering problems and more responsibility for the people solving them alongside me.

Today, I work in technical architecture. What I still enjoy most is the chance to learn something new and put it to use, whether I’m on a project at work or experimenting at home.

Engineering & leadership

How I work.

My role spans enterprise commerce, system design, delivery and developer mentorship. The decisions I make need to hold up in production and make sense to the people maintaining the software.

01

Architecture beyond the application.

I consider integrations, data models, infrastructure and deployment together. A design is only useful if it meets the business need and remains practical for the team to operate and maintain.

⌘
02

Build independent engineers.

When a developer is stuck, I try to explain how to investigate the problem rather than simply handing over an answer. I use code reviews to share context, encourage ownership and make mistakes a learning opportunity.

↗
03

Find the cause, not just the symptom.

Complex production issues rarely fit neatly inside one layer. I trace problems through application code, APIs, databases and infrastructure before deciding what needs to change.

□
04

Choose what is worth building.

I balance requirements, technical debt and delivery constraints. Sometimes the best decision is simplifying an existing implementation rather than introducing another abstraction.

◇

Selected engineering results

Work that made a difference.

Three examples from my professional experience, shared without identifying clients or internal systems.

20 min → milliseconds

Page-load performance

A page suffered an extreme loading delay. The investigation exposed oversized data transfer objects as a bottleneck. Redesigning them reduced the observed page-load time from around 20 minutes to milliseconds.

30% → 80%+

Engineering quality

My team raised JUnit test coverage from approximately 30% to more than 80%. This was a shared engineering achievement, not an individual result.

500+ stores

Retail data integration

Automated digital flyer data ingestion for a retail platform serving more than 500 stores. The work addressed an ongoing integration need at multi-store scale.

02
Electronics experiment

Motion-aware smart mirror

I built a mirror that displays useful information such as the time, calendar and weather. When the display couldn’t be controlled over HDMI-CEC, I explored using a PIR motion sensor and infrared control. Debugging the infrared signal became an experiment of its own.

  • Motion sensor
  • Infrared
  • Hardware debugging
